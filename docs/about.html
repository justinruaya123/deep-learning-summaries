HOW WE READ THE WEEK

Editorial policy

Scope

Track substantive changes in deep-learning research, model availability, training and inference systems, and evaluation or safety research. Favor technical substance over funding, personalities, stock movements, and general productivity-tool announcements.

Issues are curated reading guides, not systematic reviews or exhaustive news inventories. English-language, publicly accessible sources are favored; coverage is therefore incomplete and may overrepresent large laboratories and language models.

Dates and inclusion

  1. Use the displayed publication or release date in the original source. A dated changelog entry can establish a new integration or access change for an older model.
  2. For an arXiv paper, check its first-submission date rather than treating an in-week revision as a new paper. Pin the version read where possible.
  3. Keep events outside the stated Monday–Sunday window out of the main issue. Mention older work only as clearly labeled context.
  4. Exclude undated or inaccessible claims that cannot be verified adequately. Search snippets and weekly newsletters are discovery leads, not substitutes for reading the original source.
  5. State retrieval dates separately from event dates. Sources read after the covered week may contain later edits.

Dates follow the publisher's displayed calendar date, rather than being silently converted between time zones. A future issue may adopt normalized timestamps, but must say so explicitly.

Evidence and interpretation

Every external factual paragraph has numbered inline citations and a mechanically generated source list. Numbers are stable within an issue, not necessarily across issues. Gaps in numbering are harmless: discovery sources can be registered without being cited.

Separate three kinds of statement:

  • Announcement: what an organization says it released, changed, or made available.
  • Reported experiment: what paper authors or a vendor measured under their stated conditions.
  • Editorial interpretation: why the change could matter, labeled as interpretation rather than evidence of real-world impact.

Benchmark numbers are not independently reproduced here. Do not generalize an "up to" result into a universal gain, conflate open weights with open training data, or treat a formal proof under assumptions as proof of an entire application's security.

Verification boundaries

Checking a source means reading it and checking its stated date and support for the summarized claims. It does not mean auditing model weights, running inference benchmarks, reproducing training, rebuilding formal proofs, or validating clinical outcomes.

Public repository content must contain only the digest, editorial material, and source metadata intended for publication. Credentials, private notes, browser sessions, and raw copyrighted page captures are excluded.

Corrections and contributions

Open a GitHub issue or pull request with the affected passage, the correction, and a dated primary source. New claims need sources and must fit the issue's date window. Correct substantive errors visibly in the affected issue rather than silently changing its scope.

Preparation

The initial issue was researched and drafted with AI assistance at the repository owner's request. Sources were read during preparation; no claim of independent experimental reproduction is made. No recurring publication schedule is configured.
